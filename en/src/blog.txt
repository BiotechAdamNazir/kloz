Six Rolls, Not Sixteen!

Last Saturday, I started my first paid job at the bakery near our flat. I thought serving bread would be easy, but by the time the doors opened, I had already changed my mind. There were prices to learn, bags to fold and customers to greet. The owner, who had promised to train me, handed me an apron that almost reached my knees.

My first task was to take orders at the counter. The coffee machine was making such a loud noise that I had to ask customers to repeat themselves. One customer asked for six rolls, but I packed sixteen. Luckily, I noticed my mistake before she paid. I apologised and checked the order again, trying not to turn bright red.

The real trouble began at half past ten. We ran out of small cake boxes just as a long queue formed. There was so little space on the counter that I moved the empty trays underneath it. Then I helped my manager deal with the problem by packing the cakes in larger boxes.

When the last customer left, I felt a huge sense of relief. I had expected my feet to ache, but not my face from smiling! I've worked two more shifts since that morning, and remembering the prices is becoming easier. I'm still slow, but my manager says my confidence is growing. Next Saturday, my goal is simple: six rolls must mean six rolls.
