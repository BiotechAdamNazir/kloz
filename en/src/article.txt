A Lunch Break with a Difference

Is your lunch break becoming a routine of eating quickly and staring at a screen? At my school in Malaysia, we tried something different: a board game club. It began with just six friends, although we hoped others would join. The headteacher signed a form giving us permission to use an empty classroom during lunch. Our librarian, who enjoys puzzles herself, offered some useful advice about choosing games for beginners.

We decided to meet on Wednesdays, when no other clubs used the room. The library agreed to lend us several games, which we would return at the end of term. That meant we needed very little money to get started. Before each session, two volunteers would arrange the chairs into small circles so that nobody was left sitting alone.

At the first meeting, the rules were explained before anyone started playing. Some games took longer than we expected, so we chose shorter ones the following week. Everyone had to take turns, waiting for the previous player to finish before moving a piece. Soon, the quietest students were joining in too.

After a month, even students who had never spoken in class were chatting easily. Several said the club had given them the confidence to introduce themselves to new people. You can start something similar without buying expensive equipment: try cards or games your friends already own. Our little experiment was a success, and now Wednesday is the lunch break everyone looks forward to.
